Lecture 01 — Vertebral Column: 7 findings
Lecture_01_-_The_Vertebral_Column_-_Notes_-_With_Comments.docx
F-01 | paragraph 258 | Disease classification
Issue: The notes describe ankylosing spondylitis as a form of rheumatoid arthritis. The clinical reference identifies it as axial spondyloarthritis, a separate inflammatory arthritis. Faculty should review this classification.
Suggestion: Ankylosing spondylitis is an inflammatory arthritis within axial spondyloarthritis. It can cause spinal stiffness and, in advanced disease, vertebral fusion.
Evidence: https://rheumatology.org/patients/spondyloarthritis
Evidence: https://www.niams.nih.gov/health-topics/ankylosing-spondylitis
F-02 | paragraph 58 | Laminectomy terminology
Issue: The notes use “transection.” The clinical reference describes removal of the lamina; cutting across it alone is an imprecise definition.
Suggestion: Laminectomy is surgical removal of some or all of the lamina to create space in the spinal canal and relieve pressure on the spinal cord or nerve roots.
Evidence: https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/007389.htm
F-03 | paragraph 265 | Tectorial membrane attachment
Issue: The notes identify the atlas as the inferior attachment. Anatomical research reports attachment to the body of the axis (C2) and the cranial base. This proposed correction requires faculty review.
Suggestion: The tectorial membrane continues superiorly from the posterior longitudinal ligament, attaching to the posterior body of the axis (C2) and the cranial base of the occiput.
Evidence: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16617439/
F-04 | paragraph 125 | Hangman's fracture: neurological risk
Issue: Typical hangman fractures often widen the canal; spinal cord injury is uncommon rather than common. Atypical patterns can cause neurological injury.
Suggestion: Hangman's fracture is a traumatic fracture of the C2 pars region. Neurological injury is uncommon in typical patterns, but can occur, especially in atypical or unstable injuries.
Evidence: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4836940/
F-05 | paragraph 155 | Ilium versus ileum
Issue: The pelvic bone is the ilium; ileum names part of the small intestine.
Suggestion: Triangular shape – due to inferolateral dissipation of force through auricular surface to ilium
Evidence: https://medicine.uams.edu/neuroscience/education/medical-school-courses/human-structure-module/anatomy-tables/bone-tables/bones-of-the-pelvis-and-perineum/
F-06 | paragraph 157 | Ilium versus ileum
Issue: The pelvic bone is the ilium; ileum names part of the small intestine.
Suggestion: Auricular surface – roughened lateral surface for articulation with ilium of hip
Evidence: https://medicine.uams.edu/neuroscience/education/medical-school-courses/human-structure-module/anatomy-tables/bone-tables/bones-of-the-pelvis-and-perineum/
F-07 | paragraph 161 | Sacral foramina transmit rami
Issue: These foramina transmit spinal nerve rami, after roots have united.
Suggestion: Four pairs of anterior and posterior sacral foramina transmit ventral and dorsal rami, respectively.
Evidence: https://medicine.uams.edu/neuroscience/education/medical-school-courses/human-structure-module/anatomy-tables/bone-tables/bones-of-the-pelvis-and-perineum/

Lecture 03 — Nerves, Spinal Cord, and Meninges: 7 findings
Lecture_03_-_Nerves_Spinal_Cord_and_Meninges_-_Notes_-_With_Comments.docx
F-11 | paragraph 20 | CNS versus PNS myelination
Issue: The definition incorrectly generalizes Schwann-cell myelination to all axons.
Suggestion: Myelin is formed by Schwann cells in the PNS and oligodendrocytes in the CNS.
Evidence: https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/12-2-nervous-tissue
F-12 | paragraph 97 | Lumbar nerve labels
Issue: C1–C8 identifies cervical nerves, not the five lumbar pairs.
Suggestion: 5 lumbar pairs (L1–L5).
Evidence: https://nba.uth.tmc.edu/neuroscience/m/s2/chapter03.html
F-13 | paragraph 170 | Epidural space boundaries
Issue: External to the cord also includes intradural spaces; the epidural boundary must specify dura.
Suggestion: The spinal epidural space lies between the spinal dura mater and vertebral canal wall; it contains fat, connective tissue and venous plexuses.
Evidence: https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/13-3-circulation-and-the-central-nervous-system
F-14 | paragraph 188 | CSF circulation versus reabsorption
Issue: Sulci contain subarachnoid CSF; they are not its venous reabsorption destination.
Suggestion: CSF circulates in the subarachnoid space, including sulci, and is reabsorbed through arachnoid granulations into dural venous sinuses; other clearance pathways also contribute.
Evidence: https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/13-3-circulation-and-the-central-nervous-system
F-50 | paragraph 89 | Thoracolumbar lateral horn extent
Issue: The sympathetic lateral horn extends across thoracic segments, not just lower thoracic levels.
Suggestion: The prominent sympathetic lateral horn is classically described at T1–L2 spinal cord segments; exact boundaries vary among descriptions.
Evidence: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK545206/
F-51 | paragraph 163 | Continuous spinal catheterization is possible
Issue: An intrathecal catheter can be used for continuous spinal anesthesia; inability to catheterize is incorrect.
Suggestion: Single-shot spinal anesthesia is common; continuous spinal anesthesia is also possible using an intrathecal catheter in selected settings.
Evidence: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9463624/
F-52 | paragraph 165 | Hangover headache mechanism needs evidence
Issue: The proposed CSF-loss and dural-sagging explanation is not established here. The cited agency describes multiple contributing mechanisms.
Suggestion: Alcohol hangover is multifactorial; dehydration, inflammation and other effects can contribute. Do not equate ordinary hangover headache with CSF-leak headache without supporting evidence.
Evidence: https://www.niaaa.nih.gov/publications/brochures-and-fact-sheets/hangovers

Lecture 04 — The Shoulder: 3 findings
Lecture_04_-_The_Shoulder_-_Notes_-_With_Comments.docx
F-15 | paragraph 27 | Clavicular ossification pattern
Issue: The medial/lateral two-thirds split reverses and oversimplifies clavicular development.
Suggestion: The clavicular shaft develops predominantly by intramembranous ossification; cartilaginous end regions undergo endochondral ossification. Its curvature and cross-sectional geometry contribute to fracture vulnerability.
Evidence: https://actaorthop.org/actao/article/view/22403
F-16 | paragraph 130 | Missing posterior deltoid origin
Issue: The origin omits the scapular spine and thus the posterior fibers.
Suggestion: Origin: lateral third of clavicle, acromion and spine of scapula.
Evidence: https://medicine.uams.edu/neuroscience/education/medical-school-courses/human-structure-module/anatomy-tables/muscle-tables/muscles-of-the-upper-limb/
F-17 | paragraph 137 | Gravity and initiation of abduction
Issue: Gravity does not initiate upward arm abduction in an upright person; it opposes it.
Suggestion: Supraspinatus assists initiation of arm abduction, with deltoid contributing to abduction. In an upright person gravity opposes lifting the arm.
Evidence: https://medicine.uams.edu/neuroscience/education/medical-school-courses/human-structure-module/anatomy-tables/muscle-tables/muscles-of-the-upper-limb/

F-21 | paragraph 23 | Distal ulna and the wrist
Issue: The ulnar head articulates with the distal radius, while an articular disc separates it from the carpus.
Suggestion: The ulnar head participates in the distal radioulnar joint; the TFCC separates it from the carpal bones.

F-22 | paragraph 39 | Wrist flexors and the carpal tunnel
Issue: The rule needs an FCR exception and must distinguish retinacular structures.
Suggestion: FDS, FDP and FPL tendons traverse the carpal tunnel. FCR usually travels in a separate fibro-osseous tunnel, rather than with them.

F-23 | paragraph 93 | FDP action and joint types
Issue: FDP does not flex every hand joint, and the wrist is not a hinge joint.
Suggestion: FDP flexes digits 2–5, especially their distal interphalangeal joints, and assists wrist flexion.

F-24 | paragraph 116 | Brachioradialis insertion

F-26 | paragraph 152 | Extensor indicis origin

F-28 | paragraph 172 | Brachial artery relative to biceps tendon

Lecture 09 — The Hand: 8 findings
Lecture_09_-_The_Hand_-_Notes_-_With_Comments.docx
F-42 | paragraph 27 | Typical punching fracture
Issue: The classic boxer fracture concerns the fifth metacarpal neck, not a special third-metacarpal susceptibility.
Suggestion: Punching a hard object commonly causes a fifth metacarpal neck (boxer) fracture; other metacarpals may also be injured.
Evidence: https://www.orthoinfo.org/diseases--conditions/hand-fractures/
F-43 | paragraph 56 | FCR retinacular relationship
Issue: The superficial rule needs an exception for the usual separate FCR tunnel.
Suggestion: FCU is superficial to the flexor retinaculum; FCR usually passes through its own fibro-osseous tunnel, separate from the carpal tunnel.
Evidence: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8569728/
F-44 | paragraph 121 | Hypothenar compartment
Issue: Opponens digiti minimi belongs to the hypothenar, not thenar, compartment.
Suggestion: Deep portion of the hypothenar eminence.
Evidence: https://anatomy.ttuhscep.edu/anatomytables/muscles_alpha.html
F-45 | paragraph 168 | De Quervain versus trigger finger
Issue: These are distinct conditions, involving different tendon tunnels.
Suggestion: De Quervain tenosynovitis affects APL and EPB in the first dorsal wrist compartment. Trigger finger involves digital flexor tendons, usually at the A1 pulley.
Evidence: https://www.orthoinfo.org/diseases--conditions/de-quervains-tendinosis/
Evidence: https://www.orthoinfo.org/diseases--conditions/trigger-finger/
F-46 | paragraph 190 | Primary versus secondary Raynaud
Issue: Idiopathic applies to primary disease; Raynaud can also be secondary.
Suggestion: Raynaud phenomenon causes episodic digital vasospasm, often triggered by cold or stress. Primary disease is idiopathic; secondary disease is associated with another condition.
Evidence: https://www.niams.nih.gov/health-topics/raynauds-phenomenon
F-47 | paragraph 219 | Ulnar lumbrical motor branch
Issue: Placed under superficial-branch discussion, this wrongly implies superficial supply.
Suggestion: Lumbricals 3 and 4 receive motor supply from the deep branch of the ulnar nerve.
Evidence: https://medicine.uams.edu/neuroscience/education/medical-school-courses/human-structure-module/anatomy-tables/nerve-tables/nerves-of-the-upper-limb/
F-48 | paragraph 223 | Superficial radial nerve distribution
Issue: The branch passes to the dorsolateral hand, not into the palm superficial to palmar fascia.
Suggestion: The superficial radial nerve passes to the dorsolateral wrist and hand, supplying dorsal cutaneous branches.
Evidence: https://medicine.uams.edu/neuroscience/education/medical-school-courses/human-structure-module/anatomy-tables/nerve-tables/nerves-of-the-upper-limb/
F-53 | paragraph 207 | Median neuropathy is not inevitably irreversible
Issue: The absolute claim ignores lesion location, severity and recovery after treatment; carpal tunnel symptoms can improve.
Suggestion: Median nerve dysfunction depends on lesion location and severity. Thenar weakness or atrophy may occur; recovery varies, and persistent severe compression can cause permanent damage.
Evidence: https://www.orthoinfo.org/diseases--conditions/carpal-tunnel-syndrome/
