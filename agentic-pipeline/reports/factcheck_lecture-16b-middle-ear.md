**1. [MEDIUM · factual] Lecture 16b - Middle Ear**

- **Where:** II. External Ear > 7. Tympanic Membrane (ear drum)
- **Notes say:** "Perforation of the tympanic membrane causes deafness in the affected ear"
- **Issue:** A perforated tympanic membrane typically causes partial conductive hearing loss, not complete deafness. Overstated clinical claim.
- **References say:** Not covered by the provided OpenStax text. Verify with a standard reference.
- **Basis:** general anatomy knowledge | **Confidence:** 0.7 | **Severity:** medium
- **Second check:** confirmed: Tympanic membrane perforation typically causes only partial conductive hearing loss (at most about 40-50 dB), because the ossicular chain still conducts some sound and bone conduction remains intact, so 'deafness' and the claim that vibrations are not conducted to the ossicles overstate the effect.

**1. [MEDIUM · wording] Lecture 16b - Middle Ear**

- **Where:** II. External Ear > 7. Tympanic Membrane (ear drum)
- **Notes say:** "and then to the cochlear nerve in the cochlea of the inner ear)."
- **Issue:** Ossicle vibrations are transmitted via the stapes at the oval window to the fluid (perilymph) of the cochlea, where hair cells transduce them. They do not pass directly to the cochlear nerve; the nerve carries the signal afterwards. Misleading simplification.
- **References say:** The reference describes the stapes attached to the oval window, vibrations creating fluid waves in the scala vestibuli and scala tympani of the cochlea, with sensory neurons transducing the signal.
- **OpenStax sentence:** "As vibrations of the ossicles travel through the oval window, the fluid of the scala vestibuli and scala tympani moves in a wave-like motion." (OpenStax A&P 2e §14.1)

**2. [MEDIUM · wording] Lecture 16b - Middle Ear**

- **Notes say:** "External ear- Ear and external acoustic meatus."
- **Issue:** The external ear is described as 'Ear and external acoustic meatus', which is circular and omits the tympanic membrane.
